Active Research

Sep 2026 – Present

SPAR

Researcher · Cognitive Empathy in LLMs
  • Studying how cognitive empathy, already reported to raise model safety while avoiding deception, shows up in the hidden activations of frontier models: what its geometric structure looks like and how well it generalizes.
  • Eliciting emotion processing with carefully crafted stimuli, then reading it out with hidden-state probes, sparse autoencoder (SAE) features, and representation vectors from the interpretability literature.
  • Testing whether the resulting representations hold across languages, model sizes, model families, and architectures (dense vs. MoE), rather than being an artifact of one model.
  • Extending the study to different dialects of the same language (British vs. American vs. Indian English) to check whether the representation holds up across dialects too.

Jan 2026 – Present

Berkeley ICON Lab

Researcher · VLA Interpretability
  • Diagnosing language blindness in vision-language-action (VLA) models using linear probes on PaliGemma and action-expert hidden states.
  • Training logistic regression probes at every transformer layer on LangGap's object-replacement scenes to get an accuracy curve for each one.
  • The gap between those curves is the finding: the VLM curve rises as it encodes object identity, while the action-expert curve stays near chance, exactly where language grounding breaks down.
  • Building methods to close that gap and strengthen the action expert's grounding in instructions.

Berkeley NLP Group

Researcher · Multimodal Speech Recognition
  • Built the first audio-visual dataset that pairs speech, lip video, and accent labels at a usable scale: over 2 hours of read speech across 49 speakers, spanning eight regional dialects and eleven non-native L1s.
  • Testing whether lip-reading helps models handle accented English the same way it already helps them handle acoustic noise.

2026 – Present

Berkeley Sky Computing Group

Researcher · Agentic Search & Evaluation
  • Mining 41 evolutionary and agentic code-search traces (OpenEvolve, an in-house Evolve2, and an in-house SkyDiscover/AdaEvolve) across nine problem domains and three model families (Gemini, GPT-5, Claude).
  • Reconstructing how these searches actually move through solution space: 4,146 individual steps, each classified by execution outcome, score change, and edit size relative to that run's own baseline.
  • Crash rate looks like a property of the domain rather than the method (roughly a 10x spread across the same models and frameworks), and a single run's crash rate can swing 20+ percentage points from randomness alone, confirmed with genuine triplicate reruns.
  • Most crashes are also avoidable: 68.5% repeat an error type the same run already hit earlier in its own trace.
  • Ran an independent reward-hacking check by running a high-scoring program against seeds and noise levels it was never tuned on. Performance held flat, which looks like real generalization rather than the model gaming the evaluator.

Past Research

Dec 2025 – May 2026

Berkeley AI Sky Computing Group

Researcher · LLM Reasoning
  • Built an interpretable extension of GEPA using tuned lens for process-level evaluation of LLM reasoning.
  • Developed QThink, a latent chain-of-thought distillation method currently under double-blind review at COLM 2026.
  • QThink trains a model to reason through six silent hidden-state loops before writing an answer, distilling from the average of sixteen sampled reasoning rollouts at six checkpoints along each trace, a training signal that's both broader and denser than prior self-distillation methods.
  • Also probing what those latent states actually encode about the reasoning traces they came from.
GEPA Tuned Lens Chain-of-Thought Latent Reasoning
Nov 2025 – Mar 2026

Berkeley AI Research · Speech Group

Researcher · LLM Interpretability & RL
  • Evaluated LLM interpretability using tuned-lens methods on GPT models for multilingual multi-token prediction.
  • The logit lens's core weakness is "representational drift," where the same concept sits in a different residual-stream direction at different depths, so instead of reusing the model's unembedding matrix, I trained a small per-layer affine probe against its own final-layer output.
  • Used causal basis extraction to check whether the lens's read of a layer agrees with the directions the model's own head actually relies on, and ran depth analyses of where in the network different kinds of predictions get resolved.
  • Improved ICRL, SOAR, and TRPO for summarization and math-game tasks using note-taking mechanisms.
